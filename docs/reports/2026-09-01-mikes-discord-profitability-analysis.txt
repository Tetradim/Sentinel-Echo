IMPORTANT DATA LIMITS

Mike's positive price updates are not an unbiased trade ledger. Winning setups are
updated repeatedly, while losing setups do not always receive equivalent price
updates. Therefore, the captures cannot establish Mike's true win rate, expectancy,
or an optimal stop loss. They can establish alert grammar, demonstrated upside,
execution timing, and specific ways Echo failed to participate or exit.

The written percentage is not authoritative. It may use Mike's actual fill or DCA
average rather than the original alert header, and at least one message contained a
clear typo: SPY 766C from $0.16 to $0.25 was labeled +560%, while the premium-derived
return was +56.25%. Echo must calculate returns from its own broker-confirmed basis.

WHAT MIKE'S ALERTS ACTUALLY REPRESENT

Mike's feed is a stateful analyst conversation, not a stream of independent order
tickets. A typical lifecycle is:

1. Watch/plan: contract and levels, but no action.
2. Candidate: a full contract header may still say "$0 Entry (no fill yet)."
3. Starter fill: often a standalone price such as "$0.60 FILL (1/2 SIZE)."
4. DCA plan: "leave DCA room" or "plan to DCA" is future intent, not another buy.
5. DCA execution: "filled adds at $0.20" or a revised average is actionable context.
6. Profit updates: "HERE," "UP," and percentages are status, not new entries.
7. Scale-out: "trimming," "sold majority," "sold 80%," and "sold 10%."
8. Runner protection: "SL at B/E," "sold runners at B/E," "hands off," or "in cash."

The parser loses money when it treats any one of these messages without the preceding
same-channel contract state.

PRIMARY PROFITABILITY FAILURES

1. Quantity and replay failures dominated all other results. The August 26 loss was
   caused by 100-contract sizing and repeated buys, not by stop-loss selection.
2. Broker/local reconciliation failed around timeouts and delayed fills. Echo could
   believe an order was unfilled while Alpaca held the option, leaving it unmanaged.
3. No reliable forced 0DTE liquidation allowed 400 QQQ calls and 11 SPY contracts to
   reach expiration; one SPY call exercised into 100 shares.
4. A 10% trailing stop is only a few cents on these premiums. It repeatedly closed
   positions inside normal spread/noise before Mike's later profit updates.
5. Exact, stale entry limits missed fast winners. Orders that remained at the posted
   price for roughly two minutes were canceled after the contract had moved away.
6. Standalone fill, DCA, trim, break-even, and hands-off phrases lacked durable
   conversation binding in the historical implementation.
7. Full-position take profit conflicts with Mike's runner model. A 35% full exit
   monetizes a gain but prevents participation in the observed 75%-plus tails.
8. Malformed or edited alerts need a pending state. NOW used the wrong expiration
   before an edit; SPY 567C was likely a typo; TSLA used $0 until a later fill.

RECOMMENDED ECHO CONTROL MODEL

Priority 1 - Never permit unmanaged expiration
- Reconcile broker orders and positions continuously, with Alpaca as source of truth.
- On timeout: request cancel, then re-query the order until terminal. If a late fill
  exists, immediately adopt and manage the broker position.
- Force-close every Echo-owned 0DTE option before the exercise cutoff. Verify the
  broker position is zero. Escalate loudly if it is not.
- Never let an options alert create an equity position through exercise.

Priority 2 - Use a contract conversation state machine
- Key state by source channel plus ticker, strike, side, and expiration.
- Preserve pending candidates, confirmed fills, DCA intent, DCA fills, average basis,
  trims, runners, break-even state, and closed state.
- Bind short standalone messages only to a recent, unambiguous same-channel contract.
- If two candidates are possible, do nothing and mark the message ambiguous.

Priority 3 - Separate entry confirmation from commentary
- "$0 Entry," "no fill yet," "on watch," and "would" must never buy.
- "Plan to DCA" and "leave DCA room" change sizing intent only.
- "Filled," "in these," and an explicit add price can confirm an entry/add.
- Apply a hard per-contract quantity ceiling across starter, DCA, edits, restarts,
  mirror channels, and replayed messages.

Priority 4 - Improve entry execution without blind chasing
- Keep limit orders, but use current option quotes to reprice within a configurable
  maximum slippage band and short freshness window.
- Do not use a market order merely because the posted limit missed.
- Reject nonexistent contracts and quarantine implausible strikes/expirations until
  Mike posts a correction.

Priority 5 - Match the exit model to the alert style
- Keep trailing stop disabled during the current controlled experiment.
- Keep the current -50% stop as one test setting, not as a proven optimum.
- Make Mike's explicit sell/trim message highest-priority when the contract match is
  unambiguous.
- Replace full-position TP with a configurable partial TP. A reasonable experiment is
  70%-80% off at +35%, with the remainder protected at break-even and controlled by
  Mike's runner exit or the mandatory time exit.
- Break-even must use Echo's actual weighted broker fill basis, never Mike's text.

Priority 6 - Collect the data required to tune stops
- Record bid, ask, midpoint, last, spread, broker quantity, weighted basis, MFE, and
  MAE every few seconds for every open contract.
- Record the counterfactual outcome for stop thresholds -20%, -30%, -40%, and -50%,
  plus trailing widths 10%, 15%, 20%, and disabled.
- Compare the same fills under each policy. Do not tune from Mike's posted winners.
- Evaluate results by realized dollars, drawdown, fill rate, and exercise incidents,
  not by the analyst's displayed percentage.

CHANNEL-SPECIFIC BEHAVIOR

- Alerts: fast 0DTE lifecycle, strict time exit, conversational DCA enabled.
- Mirror analysis: strongest need for pending-candidate and standalone-fill binding.
- Mirror alerts: authoritative explicit scale-out messages; dedupe against mirrored
  copies without suppressing a legitimate first exit.
- Mirror swings/swing trades: multi-day lifecycle and separate time/stop profile.
- Watchlists, macro commentary, results recaps, and marketing: context-only, no orders.

CURRENT INTERPRETATION

The retained logs do not show that Mike's ideas are inherently unprofitable. They do
show that Echo's historical implementation converted a conversational, staged,
partial-exit strategy into oversized entries, duplicated entries, premature automatic
exits, missed fast fills, and unmanaged expiration. Execution mechanics overwhelmed
signal quality.

The highest-value next experiment is not another arbitrary stop adjustment. It is a
clean run with capped quantities, broker-confirmed state, no 0DTE expiration, improved
standalone-fill/DCA binding, quote-aware limit handling, explicit Mike exits, and full
MFE/MAE telemetry. Only then can -50% versus tighter stops be compared honestly.
